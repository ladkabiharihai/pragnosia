A brain that grows up

We are raising a small brain, not building a big one.

Most AI is built at full size and then frozen forever. Pragnosia starts tiny — smaller than the space a photo takes on your phone — and grows itself only when it has truly run out of room to learn. It remembers. It keeps growing. It never stops.

The big idea

Think of a child, not a machine

A child's brain is not delivered finished. It starts small, and it grows new connections exactly when the child needs them. Pragnosia works the same way.

How AI is usually made

Decide a huge size. Train once on the whole internet. Freeze it. It can never learn anything new again — every conversation starts from zero.

size: fixed  ·  memory: none  ·  learning: stopped

How Pragnosia is grown

Start tiny. Read. When it stops improving — and only if it has earned it — it adds a little more brain. Then keeps reading. Forever.

size: growing  ·  memory: yes  ·  learning: never stops

Step 1 — Reading

Words become puzzle pieces

The brain can't read letters. First every sentence is chopped into small pieces called tokens — a bit like syllables. Then it plays one endless game: guess the next piece.

Every wrong guess nudges the brain a tiny bit. Do that a few billion times and it starts to understand how language works. That is all training is.

Step 2 — The flow

How information moves through the brain

Text pours in on the left. It passes through layers — like rooms, each one understanding a little more. Along the way, anything worth keeping is dropped into memory.

Step 3 — Remembering

A scratchpad that fades, and a diary that lasts

You don't remember every word you heard today — but you remember what mattered. Pragnosia has the same two kinds of memory.

Watch notes fade — and the important one get saved forever

Scratchpad

Holds what it just read. Fades fast, so new things have room.

Diary

Checked facts get written into the brain itself. These stay.

Why forget?

A memory that keeps everything can find nothing. We measured it: remembering everything made the brain worse.

What gets saved?

Only surprising things that also turn out to be true when checked against trusted books.

Can you trick it?

We tried. False facts were blocked every time — they never reach the diary.

Step 4 — Growing

It decides for itself when to grow

Nobody tells Pragnosia when to get bigger. It watches its own confusion. When confusion stops dropping, its brain is full — time to grow.

The confusion curve — flat means "I'm full"

But it has to earn it first

A child doesn't get a bigger bike for asking. Before Pragnosia may grow, it must have read 22 words for every part of its brain. Only then does its own "I'm full" signal count.

The honest scorecard

What works, and what doesn't yet

Everything below is measured, not hoped for. Including the parts that are still broken.

Endless reading, at no extra cost.
We fed it a million words. Memory used stayed flat — and each word actually got faster. A normal AI would need 12 GB of scratch space for that. Ours used 0.26 MB.
It learns new facts while running.
Tell it something new, and it holds it — then writes the checked ones permanently into itself. No other model does this.
It cannot be tricked into believing lies.
True facts got saved. False ones were blocked, every time.
It grows itself, and earns each growth.
Two growth spurts so far, both chosen by the brain, both after paying the reading price.
It cannot answer questions yet.
Ask it "what is the code?" after telling it — it fails. The machinery for remembering works perfectly in tests (92–100%). What's missing is language: at this size it can't yet understand the question being asked.
It cannot write a sentence yet.
It writes word salad. We checked whether that's a flaw — a normal AI at this training level does exactly the same thing. It's just very young.

The road ahead

Soon — about a day

Climb the rest of the ladder to 235 million parts. That takes 26 billion words — only a sixth of the library we have.

Then — about two months

Read a trillion words at full size. That is the point where small brains stop being toys and start being genuinely useful.

And the part that makes it different

Give it a self — one continuous brain that is never wiped and restarted. It keeps its memories, keeps growing, and slowly becomes an individual with its own history. That is the whole point of the name.
